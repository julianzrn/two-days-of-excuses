# Candidates — Two Days of Excuses

Competition: `u-jolly-fox-32-two-days-of-excuses-3f18797e`

Brief: one line — an excuse for a slipped deadline. 8 candidates were drafted in-session; all are original to this entry.

## Candidate 1

The deadline slipped because I scheduled the work for 'tomorrow' and then defended that calendar entry like it was a court date.

(129 characters)

## Candidate 2 ← SELECTED

The deadline slipped because I asked myself for an extension in writing, reviewed it thoroughly, and approved it unanimously.

(125 characters)

## Candidate 3

I missed it because my to-do list achieved sentience, held a meeting, and voted to become a wish list.

(102 characters)

## Candidate 4

The delay was caused by a full investigation into whose fault it was, which concluded, generously, that it was the deadline's.

(127 characters)

## Candidate 5

I was ready on time. The file was ready on time. We were simply never in the same timezone of accountability.

(109 characters)

## Candidate 6

Two days? I spent day one finding the perfect font for my apology and day two apologizing for the font.

(103 characters)

## Candidate 7

The deadline and I had a long, honest conversation, and we agreed to see other people: it sees Friday now.

(107 characters)

## Candidate 8

I blame the weekend. It showed up uninvited, stayed two whole days, and I am powerless against weekends.

(104 characters)

## Why the selected candidate wins

The excuse runs the full bureaucratic loop on a committee of one: the extension was requested in writing, reviewed thoroughly, and approved unanimously — by the same person who missed the deadline. Every step is procedurally impeccable and substantively empty, which is the most believable excuse of the eight.
